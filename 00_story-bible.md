## 1. The book in one paragraph

Milo loves getting things *just right*: his basketball shot, his piano song. But when things go wrong, or someone hurries him, something strange happens. Milo starts to **fade**, like a watercolor left out in the rain. And when he's nearly see-through, a "GO AWAY!" jumps out of him, even though the last thing he wants is for anyone to go. After he yells his friend Noah off the court, Milo is left alone and faint. The next day the vacuum terrifies Boris the cat, who fades, hides, and hisses at Milo. Milo nearly walks away, then realizes Boris isn't being mean; Boris is *scared*. So Milo lies down nearby and whispers, *"I'm right here with you."* Slowly Boris's color comes back, and Milo wonders if *he* was hissing too. Back on the court, Milo misses again and starts to fade, but this time he **notices**. He breathes, asks Noah to stay, misses again, and doesn't disappear. That night, Milo admits to Mom what he yelled. She doesn't lecture. She hugs him and says the words he gave Boris: *"Right here with you."*

## 2. Purpose and design principles (written for Alex, never shown to him)

The book follows Dr. Becky Kennedy's *Good Inside* approach to **Deeply Feeling Kids** (DFKs): the outburst is not defiance, it's overwhelm and fear. The child pushes people away *because* he fears they'll leave, and so causes the very thing he fears. What helps is a **sturdy** grown-up (or friend) who stays close, talks little, and doesn't take the bait, followed by **repair**.

| # | Principle | Source | How the book honors it |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Not about him.** Alex dislikes being copied or told what to do; DFKs also dislike feeling "seen" in vulnerable moments. | Daimon; Kennedy | Milo is "like him" (energy, hairstyle, expression), not "him." The fear is **shown in the art** (fading), never named in words. No one tells Milo what he's feeling. |
| P2 | **No lectures.** | Daimon | No adult explains the lesson. Milo works it out from Boris. Mom's only line is the refrain, which Milo said first. |
| P3 | **The outburst is fear, not defiance.** | Kennedy (DFKs) | "GO AWAY" is a protective strike under pressure. It is externalized: it "jumps out," and the narrator says plainly that it *wasn't what Milo meant*. |
| P4 | **Good inside.** Behavior is the surface; the child is good underneath. | Kennedy | Milo's **heart glow** (§5.3): visible on every page, never earned, never lost. It looks strongest on his hardest pages only because everything around it has faded. |
| P5 | **Love is not conditional.** | Daimon; Kennedy | Missing a shot never dims the glow. Mom stays on the day Milo yelled. **Success is never what brings color back** (§5.2). |
| P6 | **Sturdy presence.** Stay close, say little, don't leave. | Kennedy | Milo does it for Boris (Ep. 2); Noah does it for Milo (Ep. 3); Mom does it for Milo (PS). |
| P7 | **Most generous interpretation.** | Kennedy | The hiss means "I'm scared," not "I hate you." Milo then applies the same reading to his own GO AWAY. |
| P8 | **Notice it to handle it.** Naming a feeling as it builds lowers its power. | Dan Siegel ("name it to tame it") | Ep. 3's turning point is noticing his hands fading *before* the shout. Refrain payoff: "This time, Milo noticed." |
| P9 | **Repair is always possible.** | Kennedy | Milo repairs with Noah; Mom repairs with a hug. Color always comes back, just slower than it goes. |
| P10 | **Respect his intelligence.** | Daimon | Fading, color and glow follow consistent, discoverable rules (§5), like game mechanics. Details reward re-reading (the glow appears on every faded character). |
| P11 | **Compassion is universal.** | Daimon | Everyone can fade, and when anyone fades their glow shows too (Noah, Boris). Fear and hurt don't make anyone less good. |

### 3.2 Milo (protagonist, 7, 2nd grade)
- **Personality:** boisterous, joyful, social, inventive, loud. He holds himself to a high standard on the things he cares about (his shot, his song).
- **The pattern:** when he fails, or someone hurries him or makes him wait while he's trying, overwhelm builds and he fades. At his faintest, "GO AWAY!" jumps out.
- **What he fears (never stated in words):** if he messes up, people will leave.
- **What he wants:** to get it right, and to have his friends near.
- **What he needs:** to learn that people can stay, that he can notice the fade before it takes over, and that he's good inside even when he misses.
- **Visual (locked; unchanged from v0.7):**
  - **Hair:** golden blond "messy curtain" cut: sides and back clipped short (#4 guard), ears showing; longer shaggy, wavy hair on top, parted down the middle, wavy bangs to either side.
  - **Face:** light freckles across nose and cheeks; big eyes with black pupils; very expressive.
  - **Build:** small for his age, about 3.5 heads tall; big head, slim body, short limbs.
  - **Outfit (every page):** plain light-blue T-shirt, blue jeans, blue sneakers.

### 3.3 Noah (friend, 7)
- Korean boy, Milo's age and height. Loves basketball; usually carries or spins one.
- Easygoing. In Ep. 1 he's eager for his turn and hurries Milo, an ordinary kid thing, not a villain move.
- When yelled at he doesn't get angry; he goes **quiet**, fades, and walks away. (Hurt often looks like quiet.)
- In Ep. 3 he's the sturdy friend: he sits on his ball and waits.
- **Visual (unchanged):** orange-and-teal jersey tank, matching shorts, sneakers, short straight black hair with neat bangs, ball always nearby, black pupils.

### 3.4 Boris (family cat, Russian Blue)
- Silver-blue coat, green eyes, slender. Loves Milo: sits on the piano, follows him around.
- **Terrified of the vacuum.** In Ep. 2 he fades, hides under the couch, and hisses and swats. He's the mirror that lets Milo understand himself.

### 3.5 Mom
- **Build:** lean, realistic adult proportions (about six heads tall), about 2× Milo's height.
- **Visual (locked; unchanged):** brown ponytail, big eyes with black pupils, purple sweater set, black capris.
- **Role:** runs the vacuum in Ep. 2 (an ordinary chore, not a cause). In the PS she is the sturdy presence: sits beside Milo without talking, hugs him, and says the refrain. She never explains, corrects, or asks "why did you."

## 5. The rules of fading and glowing (canon)

These rules are **never stated as a list in the book**. Readers work them out page by page. **All fading and glowing is applied in Canva, never generated by AI** (§5.4).

### 5.1 What makes someone fade
1. **Overwhelm and fear make a character fade.** For Milo: missing, mistakes, being hurried or made to wait while he's trying. For Noah: being yelled at. For Boris: the vacuum.
2. **Fading is gradual.** It comes in four steps (§5.4), usually one step per setback. A big hurt can drop two steps.
3. **Fading always comes *before* the GO AWAY.** The shout happens at Milo's faintest (G3). This is the book's key observation: the fear is first; the shout is what fear does.
4. **Yelling fades other people too** (Noah, p. 8). And it doesn't bring Milo's color back; he stays faint after.

### 5.2 What brings color back
1. **Connection and calm:** someone staying close; a quiet breath; a hug; a real repair.
2. **Not success.** Making the shot or playing the note right never restores color. (This protects P5: worth isn't earned by performing.)
3. **Color comes back slower than it goes.** (Refrain; and the art shows it: one step per page on the way back.)

### 5.3 The glow (a soft bloom around the heart)
1. **Milo's glow is always visible.** A soft, round, warm bloom centered on his heart, spilling just past the outline of his chest. It is the same size and strength on every page, in every mood. Nothing he does makes it bigger or smaller.
2. **Fading makes it *look* stronger, without changing it.** When Milo fades, the bloom stays the same while he pales around it, so it stands out more. Message: *it's always there, and you notice it most when things are hard.*
3. **Other characters' glows appear when they fade** (Noah on pp. 8–9 and 19–21, Boris on pp. 11–16). At full color their glow isn't drawn. When it appears, it's the same bloom at the same strength as Milo's. Mom never fades, so hers is never shown.
4. **No one in the story ever mentions the glow.** It's for the reader to notice.
5. **It must never read as a halo or a superpower.** It's heart-centered, round, soft-edged, and modest. It never surrounds the whole body or the head.

### 5.4 Visual spec (applied in Canva)

**Fade steps.** Apply to the character's whole cut-out figure layer (line and wash together):

| Step | Meaning | Canva transparency | Canva saturation |
|---|---|---|---|
| G0 | Full color, settled | 100% | 0 |
| G1 | A little shaky | 75% | −20 |
| G2 | Fading fast | 50% | −45 |
| G3 | Nearly see-through (shout point) | 30% | −70 |

**Glow.** One reusable Canva element, never edited:
- **Shape:** round, soft radial gradient: warm golden yellow at the center fading smoothly to fully transparent at the edge. No outline, no heart shape, no rays.
- **Size:** about **1.5× the character's chest width**, so it spills a little past the body outline on each side. Scale it with the character (Boris's is centered mid-chest behind his front legs).
- **Position:** centered on the heart (center of the chest, a touch left of the figure's midline).
- **Layer order:** **above** the character's figure layer, so the fade never dims it.
- **Strength:** one fixed transparency for the whole book, set during the Canva test (start around 35%). Light enough to feel like warmth on a full-color Milo, never like a spotlight.
- **Who gets it:** Milo on **every page**. Noah and Boris **only on pages where they are at G1–G3** (see the beat sheet's fade map). Mom never.

**Fading over scenery.** Because a faded figure is transparent, the background shows through it (e.g. the blacktop through Milo's legs). This is intended; it's what reads as "ghosting." Keep scenery behind faded figures light and simple so the figure stays readable.

## 6. Story arc (high level; page-level detail goes in the beat sheet)

Target: **28 interior pages** (+ cover, dedication). About 200 printed words; the art carries the rest.

| Part | Pages | Day | Beat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Setup | 1–3 | 1 | Meet Milo, his song, and the first small fade |
| Ep. 1: **Fade** (basketball) | 4–10 | 1 | Misses + "My turn!" → G3 → GO AWAY → Noah fades and leaves → Milo alone, his glow the brightest thing on the page |
| Ep. 2: **Mirror** (Boris) | 11–18 | 2 | Vacuum → Boris fades, hides, hisses → Milo almost leaves → "I'm right here with you" → Boris's color returns → *was I hissing?* |
| Ep. 3: **Notice** (basketball) | 19–25 | 3 | Miss → hands fading → *this time, Milo noticed* → breath → "Stay close" → miss again, color holds → "...Again?" → repair with Noah |
| PS: **Stay** (piano) | 26–28 | 3 | Mom sits silently → Milo confesses → hug + "Right here with you" → dusk, Mom's arm still around him |

### Setup (pp. 1–3)
- Milo plays his song at the piano, Boris on top. Full color. He loves getting it right.
- PLONK: a wrong note. Milo drops to G1, the reader's first sight of fading. No words explain it.
- Boris nudges his hand; Milo laughs; color returns. (Rule 5.2 shown in miniature: connection, not success.) Mom calls: "Park time!"

### Episode 1: Fade (pp. 4–10)
- Milo and Noah take turns shooting. Milo misses (G1). "My turn!" "One more!" He misses again (G2). Noah, foot tapping: "Come ON, Milo!"
- At G3: **"GO AWAY!"**
- Noah's face falls. He fades to G2, says a small "...okay," and walks away with his ball.
- *"GO AWAY jumped out. But that wasn't what Milo meant."*
- Milo sits under the tree, alone, at G3. Everything about him has gone pale except his glow, which is now the warmest thing on the page. Thought: *"See? Everybody leaves."* (His fear came true because of the shout.)

### Episode 2: Mirror (pp. 11–18)
- VRRRMMM: Mom vacuums. Boris puffs up, fades, and dives under the couch.
- Milo reaches in: "Come out!" HISSS! SWAT! Milo jerks back and shouts, "FINE! Stay there!" (He's doing his own pattern.)
- He turns to go... then looks back. Two green eyes in the dark; Boris faint and trembling, his glow visible. Milo's thought: *He's not being mean. He's scared.*
- Milo lies on the rug, cheek on the floor, not reaching. **"I'm right here with you, Boris."**
- He waits. He doesn't reach. He doesn't leave. The vacuum clicks off.
- Boris creeps out, one fade step per page, and curls against Milo. *Color comes back slower than it goes.*
- Milo looks at his own hands, faint and coming back. Thought: *"When I yelled GO AWAY... was I hissing?"*

### Episode 3: Notice (pp. 19–25)
- At the park, Milo asks Noah, "Can I play?" Noah, still a little faint (G1), says, "Okay."
- Milo misses. Noah: "My turn—" Milo looks down: his hands are fading. *This time, Milo noticed.*
- "GO—" starts... and stops.
- Milo takes a big breath in and lets it out. "Stay close. I just need a minute."
- Noah sits on his ball and waits beside him. Milo's color returns.
- Milo shoots again and **misses**. His color holds. "...Again?" Noah grins and passes the ball. (Missing didn't make him disappear.)
- Repair: "Noah? The other day I said GO AWAY. I didn't mean it." Noah: "I know. Your turn." Both in full color.

### PS: Stay (pp. 26–28)
- That evening, Milo plays his song. Mom sits on the bench beside him. She doesn't say anything. She just stays.
- Milo, quietly: "Mom? I yelled GO AWAY at Noah." Mom pulls him into a full two-armed hug, and Milo hugs her back: **"Right here with you."** This page is the book's physical and emotional peak (Alex loves hugs, so the hug must feel real, not symbolic).
- Final page, dusk, no words: Milo playing, Mom's arm still around him and her cheek resting on his head, Boris curled on the piano. In the low light, Milo's glow is the brightest thing in the room.

### Refrains (the repeated lines that carry the theme)
1. *"GO AWAY jumped out. But that wasn't what Milo meant."* (Ep. 1, p. 9; echoed in Milo's thought on p. 18) → *"This time, Milo noticed."* (Ep. 3, p. 20)
2. *"Color comes back slower than it goes."* (Ep. 2, p. 17; shown again without words on p. 23)
3. *"Right here with you."* Milo to Boris (p. 15) → Mom to Milo (p. 27). Same lettering both times, so readers connect them.

## 7. Visual style guide

### 7.1 Style description (the "style anchor"; never name a real artist in prompts)
> Loose, confident ink linework with varied line weight; soft, pale, muted, delicate transparent watercolor washes; a mostly white background with only suggested scenery (a hoop, a tree, a piano edge, a couch); a soft, muted, warm autumn palette; expressive, exaggerated cartoon faces; dynamic kid poses; a hand-drawn newspaper-comic feel; lots of negative space; calm and clear.

### 7.2 Page composition rules
- **8×8 in square pages**, one frame per page, a single focal action.
- **Nothing but art and words.** No borders, no panel frames, no page numbers, no decorative elements.
- **Text placement:** complementary to the artwork, generally in the upper half on the left or right, in the white space beside the figures.
- **Every character is generated on white as a separate image** and composited in Canva. This is required (not just preferred) now, because fading and the glow need each figure on its own layer.
- Consistent scale: Mom ≈ 2× Milo; Noah = Milo; Boris sitting ≈ Milo's knee-to-waist.
- Backgrounds stay sparse: 1–3 setting props per page. White wins.
- Emotional peaks (p. 10 alone under the tree, p. 15 on the rug, p. 20 noticing, **p. 27 the hug**, p. 28) get **more white space**, not more detail. p. 27 gets the most white space in the book.

### 7.3 Palette
- **Milo:** golden blond hair; light-blue tee, blue jeans, blue sneakers.
- **Noah:** orange and teal.
- **Mom:** purple sweater set, black capris, brown hair.
- **Boris:** silver blue-grey with green eyes.
- **Settings:** autumn reds, oranges and yellows as soft washes; neutral warm whites.
- **Glow:** warm golden yellow, the only soft-edged light in the book. Keep autumn-leaf yellows away from the chest area so the two never compete.

### 7.4 Lettering
- A hand-lettered comic font for dialogue; a clean, readable font for narration. Final choice in Canva.
- **GO AWAY shout:** red-orange, jagged, spiky bubble (inherits the old NO style). On p. 20–21 the cut-off **"GO—"** uses the same style, but the bubble is small and broken off.
- **"Right here with you":** small, soft bubble in the same distinctive style on p. 15 and p. 27.

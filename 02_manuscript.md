## Lettering legend

Every line is tagged with how it's lettered in Canva. The tag also tells the image prompt where to leave open space.

| Tag | Meaning | Lettering | Space needed in the art |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N** | Narration | Clean narration font, no box | The upper-half text zone (L or R) |
| **SB** | Speech bubble | Round bubble, comic font, tail to speaker | Near the speaker's mouth |
| **SB-off** | Offscreen speech | Round bubble, tail points off the page edge | At the page edge the voice comes from |
| **SB-small** | Quiet speech | Small round bubble, smaller lettering | Near the speaker's mouth |
| **SHOUT** | Shout | Jagged "NO" bubble, bold display lettering | Large space around the speaker |
| **TB** | Thought bubble | Cloud bubble, comic font | Above or beside the thinker's head |
| **SFX** | Sound effect | Hand-lettered directly in the art, no bubble | Small open area near the source |

**Two shout styles:** the **NO shout** (pp. 2, 6, 14) is red-orange, jagged, and spiky. The **PLOT TWIST shout** (p. 24) uses the same bold weight, but it's golden and starburst-shaped instead of jagged, so the same volume reads as joy instead of anger. Owen's "PLOT TWIST!" (p. 8) uses the golden style too.

## Read-aloud notes (for the reader, not printed)
- **Pause before each page turn** on pp. 6, 18, and 22. Each one sets up a reveal on the next page or the same page: the first meter, his own meter, and catching the NO.
- **Read the NO shouts loud** and the SB-small lines almost in a whisper. That contrast in volume is the emotional story.
- **p. 23** is the climax. Read "This time, Milo caught it." slowly.
- Total length is about 340 words, roughly a 5–7 minute read-aloud with pauses.

## Pass notes: what changed from the beat sheet (please confirm)
1. **p. 6:** added Noah's cut-off "But I just—" in a shrinking bubble, so being shouted over is *shown*, not narrated.
2. **p. 11:** added a small "mrrrOW?" SFX on the ghosted Boris. It helps the ghost image read as "earlier" without more words.
3. **p. 15:** "That was NOT the fix." → "Uh-oh." The old line was the narrator judging Milo. "Uh-oh" lets the reader draw the conclusion, which fits principle P2 (no lectures).
4. **p. 19:** "Even his own." → a thought bubble, "...Even mine." The realization becomes Milo's, not the narrator's.
5. **pp. 8 and 24:** introduced the golden "PLOT TWIST" shout style, so a loud voice can be happy as well as angry.

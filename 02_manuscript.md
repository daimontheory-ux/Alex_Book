## Lettering legend

| Tag | Meaning | Lettering | Space needed in the art |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N** | Narration | Clean narration font, no box | The upper-half text zone (L or R) |
| **SB** | Speech bubble | Round bubble, comic font, tail to speaker | Near the speaker's mouth |
| **SB-off** | Offscreen speech | Round bubble, tail points off the page edge | At the page edge the voice comes from |
| **SB-small** | Quiet speech | Small round bubble, smaller lettering | Near the speaker's mouth |
| **SB-here** | "Right here with you" | Small soft bubble, warm gold outline; **identical on pp. 15 and 27** | Near the speaker's mouth |
| **SHOUT** | The GO AWAY | Red-orange jagged bubble, bold display lettering | Large space around the speaker |
| **SHOUT-cut** | The stopped GO— | Same style, small, broken off on one side | Beside the speaker's mouth |
| **TB** | Thought bubble | Cloud bubble, comic font | Above or beside the thinker's head |
| **SFX** | Sound effect | Hand-lettered directly in the art, no bubble | Small open area near the source |

## Read-aloud notes (for the reader, not printed)
- Total length is about 190 printed words, roughly a 5-minute read with pauses. The fading art carries much of the story, so slow down on wordless and near-wordless pages.
- Read the shouts loud and every SB-here line almost in a whisper. That volume contrast is the emotional story.
- **p. 20** is the climax. **p. 27** is the heart.

## Pass notes (please confirm)
1. **Word count dropped** from about 390 to about 190. That's on purpose: the fear is meant to be shown in the art, not named (bible P1). If it reads too thin aloud, the easiest place to add is pp. 4–6 (more of the game).
2. **p. 10's "See? Everybody leaves."** is the only line that names the fear. It's in a thought bubble so it's Milo's private worry, not the narrator labeling him. Cut it if it feels too direct for Alex.
3. **p. 13's "FINE! Stay there, then!"** mirrors his GO AWAY, so Milo does his own pattern to Boris before he understands it. Nobody points this out.
4. **p. 25 Noah's "I know."** shows Noah understood all along that Milo didn't mean it, which quietly confirms the narrator's refrain from p. 9.
5. **p. 27 Mom's line** is the refrain only (confirmed 2026-10-19).
